It also showed me that sending children to a separate boarding school is not the same as making the local school include them. That is why I now aim to make Bushra able to include such learners.At Kenyatta University I was class representative for the course SCH300 in my third year. I collected classmates’ concerns and worked with the lecturers so that problems were raised early rather than left until they harmed learning. I also helped during first-year orientation. I guided new students with disabilities to their hostels and to the Directorate of Disability so they could get the facilities they needed. That work mattered to me because I use those services myself. It taught me that leadership can be practical: walking with someone and making sure they are not left alone. For these activities I received a Certificate of Leadership Development, Growth and Mentorship from the School of Education.Since 2022 I have been an incentive teacher of Mathematics and Chemistry at Bushra High School, and I was appointed Head of the Science Department. I coordinate science teachers, and I organised symposiums and contests with other schools. I also train students in sitting volleyball so learners with physical disabilities can take part in sport. The leadership with the clearest result is mentoring. I advise students on scholarships, especially the WUSC scheme to Canada. Warsame Abdikadir, whom I first encouraged as a boy of short stature facing bullying in the blocks, completed school and is now in Canada. Other Bushra students have also gained WUSC places. That showed me that one-to-one guidance, repeated over time, can change a young person’s path.Outside school I saw children in my block who needed extra help. I set up Dalha private tuition, with afternoon and evening classes in English, Kiswahili and Mathematics. I run the classes and keep families informed, including through more than 100 short educational videos. This taught me how to start a small community activity, keep it going, and work with parents.These roles taught me to start close to the problem and include people who are usually ignored. I will lead the Bushra Inclusive Education Access project in the same way, with teachers, parents and learners with disabilities.I have led in three settings: as a volunteer teacher, as an undergraduate, and as a teacher and community tutor in Hagadera.
